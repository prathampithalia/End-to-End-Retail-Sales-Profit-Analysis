### 1) Monthly Sales Trend (2022–2023)

**What**

* Sales are **highly volatile**.
* Peak: **Feb 2023 (~730K)**
* Low: **Jun 2023 (~330K)**
* That’s a **~2.2× difference** between best and worst months.

**Why this trend likely happened**

* **Seasonality**: Many retail datasets show spikes during early-year and Q4.
* **Promotions / bulk orders**: A sharp peak like Feb 2023 usually happens due to:
* discount campaigns
* corporate bulk purchasing
* major product launches


* **Demand drop / operational issues**: A deep dip like Jun 2023 could happen due to:
* fewer promotions
* supply shortage
* fewer corporate orders



**What should be done**

* Identify which drivers caused Feb 2023 spike:
* Which region? category? top products?


* Replicate the “peak month strategy” (pricing + product mix) in weaker months.
* Build a monthly forecasting + inventory plan using the historical trend.

### 2) MoM Growth % (Month-over-Month)

**What**

* Largest growth: **Feb 2023 (~+68%)**
* Largest drop: **Mar 2023 (~−46%)**
* Another major drop: **Nov 2023 (~−46%)**

This means:
Sales jumps are not sustained → growth is unstable.

**Why this trend likely happened**

* **Spike then correction** pattern:
* A promotional spike (Feb)
* followed by normal demand (Mar)


* **Q4 demand concentration**:
* Oct 2023 spike (~+49%)
* Nov crash (~−46%)
* Dec recovery (~+47%)



This often happens when:

* customers purchase early (Oct), reducing Nov demand
* or major discounting happens only in certain months

**What should be done**

* Reduce volatility by creating a **stable campaign calendar**.
* Compare **discount percent** vs sales month-by-month:
* If high sales months = high discounts → margins may be at risk.


* Track MoM by:
* region
* category
* segment
So you know where the volatility is coming from.

### 3) Category Contribution (Revenue Mix)

**What**
Revenue is balanced:

* Technology: **~35.5%**
* Furniture: **~33.5%**
* Office Supplies: **~31%**

**Why this trend likely happened**

* The business has a **diversified portfolio**.
* No single category dominates, meaning customers buy across multiple categories.

**What should be done**

* This is a good sign. But now you should check:
* **profit contribution** by category (not just sales)

Because:

* a category can contribute 35% revenue but only 15% profit (margin issue)

### 4) Top 10 Products by Sales

**What**

* Top product contributes ~**245K**
* 2nd product contributes ~**165K**
* Top product is ~**1.5×** larger than #2.

This indicates revenue depends heavily on a few SKUs.

**Why this trend likely happened**

* Certain products are:
* more frequently purchased
* higher ticket size
* heavily discounted and pushed


* Some products may be “must-have” items (especially in Technology).

**What should be done**

* Ensure stock availability for top products (avoid lost revenue).
* Check profit for these top products:
* If top-selling products have low profit margins, you may be growing revenue but not profit.


* Use cross-selling:
* Recommend accessories / related products with top SKUs.

### 5) Region-wise YoY Growth (2023 vs 2022)

**What**

* Central: **~+21%** (best growth)
* East: **~+12%**
* South: **~−6%**
* West: **~−12%** (worst)

**Why this trend likely happened**
Common reasons for region decline:

* weaker demand or competition
* lower discounting or pricing mismatch
* poor product availability
* shipping issues / delayed deliveries
* fewer corporate customers in that region

**What should be done**

* Drill down West and South by:
* category
* sub-category
* top products
* average discount
* profit margin


* Identify what Central/East are doing right:
* product mix
* discount strategy
* customer segment contribution


* Fix West/South using:
* targeted offers
* improved shipping performance
* focusing on high-profit subcategories

### 6) Profit by Sub-Category (Top vs Bottom)

**What**
Top profit drivers:

* Chairs (~155K)
* Phones (~140K)
* Binders (~100K)
* Tables (~98K)

Low profit:

* Fasteners (~1K)
* Labels, Envelopes, Art, Supplies (very low)

**Why this trend likely happened**

* Some sub-categories are:
* low ticket size
* high discount / low margin
* high shipping cost relative to price


* High profit categories are usually:
* higher unit price
* better margin
* stable demand



**What should be done**

* Focus growth efforts on high-profit subcategories (Chairs, Phones, Binders).
* For low-profit subcategories:
* reduce discounts
* bundle them with high-profit products
* consider removing poor performers if business allows


* Create a **profit margin KPI**:
* `profit_margin = profit / sales`

### Final Business Recommendations

**1) Reduce Sales Volatility**

* Sales spikes are strong but unstable → create a consistent campaign strategy.

**2) Shift from “Revenue-only” to “Profit-first”**

* Revenue is balanced across categories, but profit is concentrated in few sub-categories.

**3) Fix Declining Regions (West, South)**

* Investigate by category + discount + product availability.

**4) Protect Top Products**

* A few products drive large revenue → ensure availability + check margins.

**5) Improve Low-Profit Subcategories**

* Reduce discounts, bundle, or deprioritize low-margin items.
